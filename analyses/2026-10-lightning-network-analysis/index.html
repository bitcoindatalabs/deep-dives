Lightning Security On-chain May – October 2026 · TABConf 2026

The Summer Lightning Broke (a Little)

Between July and October every Lightning implementation shipped security fixes, and Core Lightning's maintainers asked operators to restart their nodes offline until a fix shipped. A fifth of CLN nodes went dark within a day, force closes on their channels doubled, and half were back within a week. The rest of the network barely noticed: what decides how far a client bug reaches is whether it hits the hubs, not how many nodes run that client. Everything below comes from daily gossip snapshots and our own Bitcoin node.

What happened

    Dates are those of the public posts (UTC); each links to its source. The Jul 10 event is not a Lightning bug: we saw it in gossip — channels toward Tor-only nodes of every client were disabled at once — during a denial-of-service wave against Tor that cut the relays serving onion-service descriptors from 3,874 (Jul 9) to 1,780 (Jul 12). The BTCPay incident was an application-layer credential leak (macaroons), not an LND protocol bug. CLN's Aug 26 advice was to upgrade or restart with --offline, which keeps the node running and watching the chain but drops every peer; it circulated during the day and was posted publicly at 23:26 UTC.
