Quick one. Take a vote, then reveal.

Answer: about 6×. Median 209 sat cooperative vs 1,222 sat all-in for a force close (the close plus the follow-up sweeps).

"The fees aren't the real pain. With a force close, your own money is stuck behind a time-lock for days." Across all force closes since May: about 0.17 BTC in fees.

Lightning in 60 seconds

Payments hop through channels, often through a hub

Gossip

Nodes announce their channels. That public map is what I recorded every day.

Cooperative close

Both sides sign. One cheap transaction. Money is free right away.

Force close

One side closes alone. Higher fees, and the money waits out a time-lock.

For the newer folks; experts will forgive you. Keep it under a minute.

"A channel is bitcoin two people lock together on-chain. Payments hop across channels, and in practice they often go through big, well-connected nodes: hubs."

Click through the three cards. "Gossip is the public map. Cooperative close is the polite exit. Force close is the messy one."

Ask: "Quick check: if your peer disappears, which close are you stuck with?" (Force close.)

Who listened most? The biggest nodes (32%) and Tor-only nodes (28% vs 7% for clearnet-only). LND, LDK, Eclair: no change.

Vote, then reveal. Option A is the trap: 2% is just a normal day's churn.

Answer: 22%. About ten times an ordinary day. Peers saw it too: the share of channels toward CLN nodes marked disabled jumped from 12% to 38%.

Ask: "Is 22% a lot or a little for an emergency call?" Let someone answer. Both views are fair: most operators patched instead of going offline.

Second click: the largest fifth of CLN nodes complied most (32%). Other clients didn't move, so this was contained to one client.

The Oct 2 "active attacks" warning produced no second wave. If short on time, skip this slide with one sentence.

Guess the spike

Force closes per day. Are these spikes attacks?

Each spike is one operator closing its own channels. Without them, force closes on CLN channels still ran 2.2× normal for two weeks (LND-only: 0.8×).

Ask: "What do you think these tall spikes are? Hacks? Panic?" Let a couple of people guess.

Click: each spike is one node force-closing many channels in a day. NiceHash: 226 on Aug 3. Kraken: 101 on Aug 12 while moving its node. Wallet of Satoshi: 205 on Aug 26, the same day as the CLN call. That's a coincidence that could fool a quick analysis.

Second click, the real signal: with those operators removed, organic force closes on CLN channels ran 2.2× their July–August rate for two weeks.

Lesson: "When you see a spike on a chart, ask who's behind it."

Vote, reveal. Answer: 38%, against about 18% in the same-length window starting May 1. 44% of the capacity.

The grid: each square is 1% of the channels. The outlined ones are gone.

"Does that mean people fled Lightning?" Not really: most closes were cooperative, and ten operators were party to almost a third of them, led by Kraken moving its node. Client shares barely moved.

Explain reachability in one breath: "pick any two nodes; can a 100k-sat payment get from one to the other?"

Vote, reveal. The Tor wave cost twice as much: 15.6–16 points vs 7.8 for the CLN shutdown. A normal two days costs about 3.6.

"The thing that hurt most this summer wasn't a Lightning bug. It was the network underneath Lightning."

Honesty note if asked: 4 of 94 ordinary windows cost more than the CLN shutdown (early-August closing waves).

Callback to Quiz 1: "Eclair: 42 nodes, 6% of the bitcoin. This is why."

The critical list is almost all LND, simply because the hubs run LND. Client diversity counted by nodes doesn't tell you where the risk sits.

Three things to take home

1

Count isn't resilience

The #2 client went offline and the damage stayed contained to it.

2

Hubs set the blast radius

One well-connected node outweighs every Core Lightning node combined.

3

Look under the client

A Tor attack cut more routes than any Lightning bug this summer. And when a chart spikes, ask who's behind it.
