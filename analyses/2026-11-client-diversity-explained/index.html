Does client diversity protect the Lightning Network?

A short guide to our paper on the August 2026 Core Lightning shutdown, and the data behind it.

  • The paper (arXiv)
  • Code and data
  • Companion: who patches Lightning?

Summary in plain words

Client diversity protects the network only where it reaches the hubs.

  • 1LND runs 80% of public nodes and holds 84% of capacity. One operator's fleet of about 2,000 tiny nodes hides LND's falling node share.
  • 2After CLN's 26 August call to upgrade or go --offline, 22% of CLN nodes went dark, about ten times an ordinary day. Other clients didn't move.
  • 3The rest of the network lost 7.8 points of reachability: about an unusual but ordinary two days, and half what July's Tor attack cost.
  • 4ACINQ's Eclair node alone matters four times more than all 619 routable CLN nodes, and 19 of the 20 most critical nodes run LND.
  • 5CLN leans on LND about as much as its size implies; CLN operators peer with each other more than chance would.

1 LND dominates, and raw node counts hide how much

Client labels come from each node's announced feature bits. LND runs 80% of public nodes and holds 84% of public channel capacity. CLN has many small nodes; Eclair a few very large ones.

One operator props up LND's node count

From July 2025, one operator ran a fleet of about 2,000 tiny LND nodes, a fifth of all public nodes with 0.02% of the capacity. Without it, LND's node share fell from 85–87% in late 2024 to 80%.

Labels are checked against 795 self-identifying aliases and against each node's funding-wallet fingerprint. Both agree for at least 97% of LND and CLN nodes.

2 On 26 August, a fifth of Core Lightning nodes went dark

  • 10 JulDenial-of-service wave against Tor.
  • 11 AugLightning Labs publishes ten LND advisories.
  • 26 AugCLN maintainers: upgrade, or restart with --offline.
  • 28 AugCLN 26.06.7 ships.
  • 2 OctCLN warns that attackers target nodes on 26.06.7 or earlier.

We measure outages from peers' disabled flags. The disabled share toward CLN nodes rose from 11.8% to 38.1% on 27 August; LND, Eclair and LDK stayed flat.

Half the dark nodes were back within a week, after 26.06.7 shipped; a third were still dark five weeks later. Organic force closes on CLN channels ran at 2.2 times their July–August rate for the next two weeks.

3 The rest of the network barely noticed

Reachability here is the share of node pairs connected by a path that can carry 100,000 sats. The shutdown cut it by 7.8 points. Of 94 ordinary two-day windows from May to August, 4 lost more; the July Tor attack lost twice as much.

Inside CLN it was different: pairs of CLN nodes lost 24.5 points, more than in any ordinary window.

4 Hubs, not cohorts, decide how far a failure reaches

CLN nodes sit mostly at the edges of the graph. Removing the 300 largest nodes one at a time, ACINQ's Eclair node alone costs the remaining nodes 15.9 points, four times as much as all 619 routable CLN nodes together.

5 Minority clients lean on LND about as much as their size implies

76% of CLN's capacity faces LND. Shuffling client labels 200 times among nodes of similar size, chance alone would put 88% there. CLN operators peer with each other about twice as often as chance, but that backbone is too small to stand alone.

Takeaways

  • Track which software the hubs run, not how many nodes run each client.
  • Watch peers' disabled flags per client and per transport. They are cheap to compute and showed both the CLN shutdown and the Tor attack clearly.
  • Emergency shutdowns cost the operators who follow them. Scope them by version and duration, and prefer --offline.
  • Beyond Lightning (our reading): where a few nodes carry most of the traffic, the software those few run likely matters more than the overall mix.

Common misreadings

  • "Lightning lost a fifth of its nodes."A fifth of CLN nodes, about one public node in ten.
  • "The security bugs shrank the network by 38%."Channels of every client fell at similar rates from August to October; large operators' exits drove it.
  • "ACINQ's node failed."It didn't. The 15.9 points model what its loss would cost.
  • "Home nodes on Tor obeyed the call."Tor-reachable nodes complied most, but by capacity the largest fifth did (32%). Who ran them is unconfirmed.
  • "LND runs 84% of nodes."80% without one fleet. It holds 84% of capacity.

How we measured it

  • Data: 805 daily gossip snapshots from our own LND node (May 2023 to 4 October 2026); 132,360 channel closes and 226,134 funding transactions from our own Bitcoin Core node.
  • Clients: feature-bit rules, checked against self-identifying aliases and funding wallets.
  • Outage: peers' disabled flags. A node is dark when at least 80% of the channels toward it are disabled.
  • Reachability: 100,000-sat paths in the routable graph of 25 August (4,435 nodes).

What this doesn't show

  • One node's view of the public graph. Private channels, where most LDK and Phoenix use happens, are invisible.
  • Connectivity overstates payment success. Every comparison here uses the same yardstick.
  • Patch versions, or why operators complied. Our companion paper adds release lines.
