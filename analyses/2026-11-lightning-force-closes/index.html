What does it cost to force-close a Lightning channel?

A short guide to our paper on 973,581 Lightning channel closes from 2018 to October 2026, and the data behind it.

Summary in plain words

Fee spikes do not bring waves of force closes. A force close costs a multiple of a cooperative close and days of locked funds; when fees spike, payments run late, but few are lost.

1 Fee spikes do not bring a wave of force closes

A spike week has a median block fee rate at least twice that of the previous eight weeks, and at least 10 sat/vB. In the nine spike weeks and the weeks after, organic public force closes were 0.97 times their level in the four weeks before. After ordinary weeks of the same years the ratio is typically 1.02, so the spikes sit at the 23rd percentile, below a typical week. Pre-anchor channels, whose fee is fixed when it is signed, show the same (0.98).

The chain shows confirmation, not broadcast. A close broadcast during a spike at too low a fee would confirm in the weeks after it. Those weeks show no rise beyond what ordinary weeks produce either.

2 A force close costs two to six times a cooperative close, and days of locked funds

In fees alone a force close is cheap: the median public force close cost 925 sat in 2026 and 14,318 sat in 2020, the dearest year. Against a cooperative close of the same year it cost two to six times as much, in every year. The closer's own balance comes back a median 1.7 days after the close, and in the slowest tenth of closes up to 22 days.

To set the wait against the fees, the paper uses a break-even rate: the yearly return on the locked funds at which the wait costs as much as the fees. It was 2.7% in 2025 and 1.2% in 2026, but 6.1–54.1% before 2025, so the wait is the larger cost only when fees are low.

3 When fees spike, HTLC timeouts run late, but few payments were lost

In every year the median HTLC timeout confirmed 1–3 blocks after its expiry. The tail grows with fees: in 2023, 18.2% confirmed more than 40 blocks late, the tenth percentile of the margins public channels advertise. When the block fee rate at expiry was 50 sat/vB or more, 30.5% of pre-anchor timeouts were that late, against 20.6% of anchor ones: the effect anchors were designed for.

Late is not lost. Since 2021, forwarding nodes lost 38 payments (0.44 BTC) by paying out downstream while the upstream HTLC timed out. In every one the preimage was already on-chain, a median 58 blocks before the upstream timeout, so the node failed to act rather than being outbid.

4 Owners abandoned their anchors, and no third party sweeps taproot ones

Owners spent 81% of the P2WSH anchors of 2022's force closes and 5% of 2026's, mostly as cleanup rather than fee bumps. Third parties sweep the rest, and none of the 387,502 anchors is left unspent. Sweeping barely pays: of 0.95 BTC swept since 2022, 0.75 BTC went to miners in fees.

Simple-taproot anchors are the exception: 22,158 of 23,864 are unspent, and no third party has swept one. The key a sweeper needs appears on-chain only when the matching output is swept, but for 14,828 unspent anchors it has been public for a median 101 days. An open LND proposal to stop sweeping dangling anchors (#8997) assumes third parties will; that holds for P2WSH anchors only.

Zero-fee commitments save 12% of the anchor mechanism's block space, not of Lightning's. Their gains lie elsewhere: no 330-sat outputs left to sweep, no fee guessed at signing, and no dependence on the mempool rule Bitcoin Core 31.0 removed.

5 Force closes became rarer per channel, and most now happen off the public graph

Of the public channels funded in 2019, 35% were force-closed within a year; of those funded in 2025, 17%. Unlike a rate per open channel, this measure is not inflated by abandoned channels. Most force closes now happen off the public graph: in 2026, unlisted force closes outnumbered public ones 3.4 to one. Of 2026's public force closes, 26% still used the pre-anchor format, but five nodes were party to 74% of those; without them the share was 8%.

Takeaways

  • Judge zero-fee commitments by HTLC deadlines, not block space. The baseline: at 50 sat/vB or more, 20.6% of anchor channels' HTLC timeouts confirmed over 40 blocks late.
  • Treat taproot anchors apart when changing anchor sweeping (our reading). No third party sweeps them, so a node that stops sweeping its own leaves them in the UTXO set.
  • For forwarding nodes, staying online matters more than outbidding (our reading). Every forwarder loss since 2021 had the preimage on-chain before the upstream timeout.
  • Compare rates, not totals. Public force closes fell from 57,896 in 2022 to 15,440 in January–October 2026, partly because the network shrank.

Common misreadings

  • "No wave means fee spikes don't hurt Lightning."They hurt after the close: in 2023, 18.2% of HTLC timeouts confirmed more than 40 blocks late.
  • "Late HTLC timeouts mean forwarders lost money."Since 2021, 38 payments (0.44 BTC) were lost, each with the preimage already on-chain.
  • "Zero-fee commitments cut Lightning's block space by 12%."They cut the anchor mechanism's block space by 12%: 173 to 152 vB per close.
  • "Private channels force-close 3.4 times as often as public ones."It is a count: private channels' cooperative closes are invisible, so no rate exists.
  • "A week of 306 penalties means cheating on Lightning rose."305 match a Core Lightning splicing bug fixed in v26.06.8 on 19 September, before the wave: a close at the moment a splice locked broadcast a commitment that was soon revoked.

How we measured it

  • Chain: every block from 12 January 2018 to block 974,041 (31 October 2026), from our own Bitcoin Core node.
  • Closes: public channels from our gossip since May 2023, mempool.space and the lnresearch archive (dense from January 2019, so public series start then). A commitment's fingerprint in its locktime and input sequence finds private channels' force closes too; each of their 1,346,588 outputs is followed to its spend.
  • Validation: a separate index of the chain agrees on all 698,476 public closes compared; mempool.space's close reasons agree on 100.00% of the 480,440 it labels.
  • Fee spikes: judged against ordinary weeks of the same years; organic force closes leave out days on which one node was party to 20 or more.

What this doesn't show

  • Which side broadcast a force close.
  • Fee bumps that never confirmed, and private channels' cooperative closes.
  • Simple taproot channels' delays and HTLCs: their spends are not classified.
